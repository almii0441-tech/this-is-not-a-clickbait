01 / THE REFLEX

In my recent doomscrolling endeavours I came across an interesting trailer for the upcoming movie Artificial. A biopic on Sam Altman, the CEO of OpenAI. The plot seems to primarily focus on the crisis of leadership and the high stakes of the developing global artificial intelligence race while possibly also portraying the risks of it. But before I could even grasp the themes around which the movie is centered on, I was curious to see how people were receiving the trailer and to my surprise it was all judgement and no curiosity. The comments argued “people need to stop making biopics before the person is dead”, “ok so we are pretending like he is important”, “yea nobody wants to see this”. No one had even tried to examine the important themes or the art behind the craft of filmmaking before branding it as an unimportant or failed movie. Before trailers used to be an invitation to a movie, to give people just enough information as to what the movie is about and excite them about the themes about to be displayed. But the overwhelming amount of content one consumes on their social media feeds on a daily basis makes them look at trailers as yet another content piece, overly critiquing them and assuming how bad or good the movie will be by just a 1 minute clip and nothing more. So is this over consumption and over exposure to short form content making us more critical and quick to judge a creative piece?

“We become reactive towards art rather than reflective.”
02 / THE OVERLOAD

Firstly, we must establish that we are in an era where making any piece of art or any type of content is easier and more accessible. This knowledge gives us a green card to dismiss it and critique it before even analysing the content in its entirety because we know the process was easier compared to that of a person producing something creative a decade or a century ago. We become reactive towards art rather than reflective. This is a level of desensitisation that comes with technological development and over flow of productions and distributions.

In the past decade we have seen so much evolution in specifically film media, that it takes quite a lot of creative experimentation to make us in awe of it. Psychology talks about this phenomenon called Hedonic adaptation, which states how people get desensitised or ‘used to’ big events and stimuli in their life thus lowering their happiness baseline over time. Constant stimulation dulls our response over time, making us expect more novelty to appreciate anything. These expectations can become ignorant to meaningful human art. This can even create a chain reaction creating more critical masses as addiction to social media makes it our second nature to look at reviews and others opinions before we even form ours. We tend to remember and weigh negative information more in our minds as a form of negative bias as discussed in psychology. This negative bias makes us go down a rabbit hole of only harsh critiques and quick dismissals comments compared to genuine curiosity and openness and deeper analysis of media and art.

03 / THE SECOND VERDICT

A Bollywood sci-fi movie called Ra.One starring Shah Rukh Khan was released in 2011. Initially receiving harsh reviews and critics, comparing it with Hollywood super hero movies and characters, predicting its failure in the box office yet it went on to make 270 crore rupees worldwide against a 150 crore rupees budget. Years later, I came across multiple edits of the movie on my reels feed praising it for its VFX effects, many even claiming it to be the best visual effects in Bollywood’s history and asking for a sequel in the comments section and Reddit posts. Ra.One shows what distance adds to a first verdict. The first verdict was formed in a release-week frenzy, and the second came with time to compare, revisit and look more closely.

A first reaction is not always a final thought.
04 / THE REFLECTION

This over exposure of content conditions us to be more skeptical towards creative pieces which can serve a good purpose if practiced consciously and genuinely. But people seem to adapt this attitude because being unimpressed suddenly looks smart and over qualified for their niche interests. We no longer see many people consuming content and media to become more discerning but rather to be undermining. We seem to give in verdicts before even watching a single frame of the movie. Even when we consume a movie or a creative piece in its entirety we are quick to pass judgement and move on from it because there is an overflow of content to keep up with and watch next, we no longer sit with our thoughts or feelings the content evoked in us to truly know our personal review of it and what it means to us.
